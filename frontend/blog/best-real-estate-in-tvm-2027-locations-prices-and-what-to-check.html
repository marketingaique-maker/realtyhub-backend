Best Real Estate in TVM 2027: Locations, Prices and What to Check Before You Buy

Admin User · 19 Nov 2026

Best real estate in TVM is one of the most common searches we hear about from homebuyers and investors right now, and it’s easy to see why. Thiruvananthapuram is adding IT campuses, a working international port and major transport projects, and each of these could change where people want to live and invest over the next few years.

In this guide, we’ll break down what’s shaping the Trivandrum property market heading into 2027, what the official price data actually shows, which localities are worth a closer look, and how to approach a purchase whether you’re a first-time buyer, an NRI, or a long-term investor

What’s Shaping Trivandrum’s Property Market in 2027

Trivandrum is mostly an end-user market. Most people buy here to live, not to flip, which keeps it steadier than more speculative cities. A few big drivers are worth understanding before you buy:

  • Technopark Phase IV (Technocity):
A 389-acre integrated township combining IT campuses with residential, commercial and research zones. Technopark expects it to generate about 200,000 jobs over the long term, and a TCS campus is being developed on 94 acres there. therealtytodaytechnopark

  • Taurus Downtown (Technopark Phase III):
Under construction, with 55.58 lakh sq. ft. of IT space planned across two phases, more jobs right inside the existing Kazhakkootam belt. technopark

  • Vizhinjam International Seaport:
Commercial operations started on December 3, 2024, and full export-import (EXIM) operations began in August 2026. Road and rail links to the port are still being built. onmanorama

  • Thiruvananthapuram Metro:
A 31 km, 27-station Phase 1 corridor from Pappanamcode to Enchakkal, which received in-principle approval in November 2025. It includes Technopark, but the detailed project report was still awaiting approval in mid-2026, so trains are not a 2027 story.

  • Outer Ring Road (ORR):
A planned 62.2 km, six-lane highway around the city. The government has said it will allocate ₹3,200 crore for land acquisition. This is a long-term factor for land along the route.

The takeaway: the job drivers near Technopark are already underway, while the transport projects are still years from finished. Buy on what exists today, and treat future projects as a bonus.

Price Trends & ROI Potential

Two official indices track house prices in Trivandrum, and they measure them differently:

NHB RESIDEX (valuation-based): In January,March 2025, Thiruvananthapuram was one of only two cities out of 50 to record a price decline. In the following quarter it recorded a 4.8% fall.

RBI House Price Index (transaction-based): In April–June 2026, Thiruvananthapuram was one of the main cities driving a 1.1% quarterly rise in the all-India index, and was named among the contributors to 3.6% annual growth.

So the official picture is a softer 2025 followed by firmer movement in 2026. Because the two indices use different methods, read them as signals of direction, not exact figures. (RBI · NHB RESIDEX)

How 2027 could play out:

  • If Technocity fills up and port activity grows:
Demand along the Kazhakkootam–Technocity corridor and the south could strengthen.

  • If projects move slowly:
Prices are likely to stay broadly stable and follow wider economic conditions.

  • Risks to watch:
Interest-rate changes, project delays, too many new apartments in one pocket, and slower IT hiring.

Best Locations to Invest in Trivandrum in 2027

There’s no single “best” locality. The right one depends on your budget, whether you’ll live there or rent it out, and how long you plan to hold.

Kazhakkootam: Next to Technopark and on NH66, with the strongest rental demand from IT professionals. Check junction traffic and drainage in low-lying pockets.

Technocity corridor (Pallippuram, Mangalapuram): An early-stage area tied to future IT jobs. Mostly plots, suited to long-term land buyers.

Karyavattom: Close to the University of Kerala campus and Greenfield International Stadium. Popular with families and academics.

Sreekaryam: On the city–Technopark route, near College of Engineering Trivandrum, with steady rental demand from students and faculty.

Akkulam: Quieter, with easy access to the city, Technopark and the airport. Check flood mapping near the lake before buying.

Peroorkada and Vattiyoorkavu: Settled neighbourhoods favoured by government employees and families. Land supply is limited.

Kowdiar: A premium central address near top schools and hospitals. High entry prices and little new land.

Vizhinjam–Kovalam belt: The port and tourism side of the city, suited to long-horizon investors. Check CRZ rules and real progress on port connectivity.

Apartments, Villas, Plots or Commercial: Which Fits You?

  • Apartments:
Security, amenities and easier renting near job hubs. Factor in maintenance charges, and check K-RERA registration for new projects.

  • Villas and independent houses:
More space, privacy and land ownership. Upkeep costs more, and resale can take longer.

  • Plots:
Flexibility to build later, with no building depreciation. A plot earns no rent while it sits, and title, road access and land classification need careful checks.

  • Commercial spaces:
Longer leases and business tenants, but a unit can sit empty if nearby office demand slips.

No property type guarantees appreciation or rental returns. What you pay and where you buy matter more than the category.

Who Should Invest in Trivandrum in 2027

Trivandrum isn’t a one-size-fits-all market. Different buyers come here for different reasons:

IT Professionals (25–35)

Buying a first home or flat, prioritising a short commute to Technopark.

Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)

Looking for a stable Kerala asset that can be rented to the IT workforce while they’re abroad.

Families & Upgraders

Moving from a rented flat to a house or larger plot, often a little further from the main junctions.

Long-Term Land Bankers

Buying land along the Technocity or southern corridors and holding it for years, not months.

If you’re an NRI, it’s worth reviewing Kerala’s registration and stamp duty process in advance, since documentation can differ slightly for overseas buyers

Is Trivandrum a Good Bet for 2027?

For medium- to long-term buyers, Trivandrum has a lot going for it: a diverse job base across government, IT, healthcare and education, steady end-user demand, and real infrastructure progress.

It’s less suited to anyone expecting quick, guaranteed gains. Official data showed price dips as recently as 2025, rental yields are often modest, and several big projects are still at the approval or land-acquisition stage. Go in with a clear purpose and a realistic timeline.

How to Choose the Right Property in Trivandrum

A few practical checks before you pay any advance:

Title and encumbrance: Trace the title chain, verify survey numbers and get an encumbrance certificate.

Land classification: Confirm the land isn’t restricted paddy or wetland, and check its permitted use.

Approvals: Look for a building permit and occupancy certificate for built property, and K-RERA registration for new projects.

Road access and frontage: Direct, all-weather road access makes a property easier to build on and resell.

Exact distances: “Near Technopark” can mean 1 km or 6 km, so ask for the actual distance.

Registration costs: For most sale deeds in Kerala, stamp duty is generally 8% and the registration fee is 2%, calculated on the property’s fair value or the sale price, whichever is higher. Confirm the current rates with the Kerala Registration Department.

Legal review: Have a qualified property lawyer check the documents before you sign.
