Documents Required to Buy Land in Kerala: A Complete Checklist (2026)

Buying land in Kerala can be a straightforward process but only if the paperwork is right. The most common (and most expensive) mistakes buyers make aren't about picking the wrong plot; they're about skipping a document check that later turns into a legal headache.

This guide walks through exactly what documents are required to buy land in Kerala, the registration process, and a few things NRI buyers in particular should watch for.

Why Document Verification Matters Before Buying Land

A plot can look perfect:good road access, close to Technopark, priced fairly and still come with hidden problems: an unclear title, an unpaid loan against the property, or a family dispute over inheritance. None of these show up in photos or a site visit. They only show up in the paperwork.

Verifying documents before you pay any advance isn't just a formality, it's what protects you from buying a dispute along with the land.

Complete Document Checklist

Before finalizing any land purchase in Kerala, confirm the seller can provide the following:

Title Deed — the primary legal document proving ownership, showing the chain of previous owners

Encumbrance Certificate (EC) — confirms the land has no pending loans, mortgages, or legal disputes attached to it

Possession Certificate — confirms who currently holds physical possession of the land

Tax Receipts (Land Tax) — proof that property tax has been paid up to date, with no dues pending

Survey Sketch / Plan — an official map showing the plot's exact boundaries and dimensions

Building Permit (if the plot has existing structures) — confirms any construction was legally approved

RTC / Thandaper Document — a Kerala-specific land record showing ownership and land classification details

Succession Certificate (if the land is inherited) — establishes legal right to sell in cases of inherited property

Power of Attorney (if applicable) — required when someone other than the owner, such as a representative for an NRI seller or buyer, is completing the transaction

Step-by-Step Registration Process in Kerala

  • Once documents are verified, the actual registration process generally follows these steps:
  • Both parties agree on the sale price and draft a sale agreement
  • Stamp duty is calculated based on the property's value and paid in advance
  • Buyer and seller visit the Sub-Registrar Office with all original documents, ID proof, and photographs
  • Biometric verification (fingerprint and photo) is completed for both parties
  • The registration fee is paid, and the sale deed is officially registered
  • A certified copy of the registered document is issued to the buyer

Stamp Duty and Registration Charges in Kerala

Stamp duty is a state government tax paid at the time of registration, calculated as a percentage of the property's value, along with a separate registration fee. These rates are revised periodically by the state government, so rather than relying on a fixed figure, it's best to confirm the current applicable rate directly through the Kerala Registration Department (IGR Kerala) before budgeting for your purchase.

Special Considerations for NRI Buyers

NRIs purchasing land in Kerala from abroad face a few additional steps:

Power of Attorney (PoA): commonly used to authorize a trusted representative in India to complete document verification and registration on the buyer's behalf

Remote coordination : while physical presence isn't always required if a valid PoA is in place, buyers should confirm requirements with the specific Sub-Registrar office handling the transaction

Repatriation basics : if you plan to eventually sell and transfer proceeds abroad, it's worth understanding RBI guidelines on repatriation before you buy, not after

Common Documentation Mistakes to Avoid

  • Paying an advance before the Encumbrance Certificate has been checked
  • Assuming verbal assurances from the seller are enough always ask for original documents
  • Not checking whether the land is inherited property with multiple legal heirs who all need to consent to the sale
  • Ignoring small mismatches between the title deed, tax receipts, and RTC/Thandaper records these inconsistencies should always be resolved before payment

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Why is an Encumbrance Certificate important when buying land in Kerala?

An Encumbrance Certificate (EC) confirms that the land is free of legal or financial liabilities such as unpaid loans, mortgages, or ongoing litigation. Without a clear EC, you risk inheriting the seller's outstanding debts or legal disputes tied to the property.

  • How long does land registration take in Kerala?

Once all documents are verified and stamp duty is paid, registration at the Sub-Registrar Office is usually completed within a single day, though document verification and preparation beforehand can take one to a few weeks depending on the property's history.

  • Is Power of Attorney mandatory for NRIs buying land in Kerala?

It is not always mandatory, but a Power of Attorney is commonly used by NRIs who cannot be physically present for the registration process, allowing a trusted representative in India to complete the transaction on their behalf.

  • What happens if some documents are missing or incomplete?

Missing or incomplete documents can delay or invalidate a land purchase, and in some cases lead to disputes later. It's important to verify the full document set before making any advance payment, ideally with the help of a lawyer or an experienced local agent.

  • How do I verify a title deed before buying land?

Title deed verification typically involves checking the chain of ownership at the Sub-Registrar Office, confirming the seller's name matches records, and cross-checking with the Encumbrance Certificate and RTC (Thandaper) document to ensure consistency.

  • What is stamp duty and how much is it in Kerala?

Stamp duty is a state government tax paid at the time of property registration, calculated as a percentage of the property's value. Rates can change periodically, so it's best to confirm the current rate directly with the Kerala Registration Department before budgeting for a purchase.
